Built around how Maryland eats

Star ratings won't tell you which crab house Baltimore argues over or which snowball stand is open on a July night. Maryland Eats is organized the way people here eat: crab cakes, pit beef, oyster bars, pupusas, Ethiopian, Koreatown and snowballs, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Montgomery County places show the county's own latest inspection result, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on the water.

What stays on your phone

Maryland Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Maryland Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The crab cake, pit beef, oyster bar, pupusería, Ethiopian, Koreatown, snowball and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: a dated magazine or news story or an official tourism listing. The wider directory of 12,501 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against the food facilities Montgomery and Anne Arundel counties inspect, and adds inspected restaurants the map data missed.

Where can I get a crab cake?

Our crab cake list has 11 hand-checked places around Baltimore, from Faidley's at Lexington Market, selling them since 1886, to crab houses in Dundalk and Linthicum. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Maryland restaurant's inspection?

Each of Maryland's 24 local health departments inspects its own restaurants, and none posts a letter grade. Montgomery County publishes every inspection with its result (Pass, Fail, Closed), so the app shows the latest one for Montgomery places, in the county's words and dated. Elsewhere results aren't published as data, so each place links to the list of health departments, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

In Montgomery County and Baltimore City, when the county says so: a place holding an on-premises alcohol license there shows it. The other counties' liquor boards publish no list we may read, so anywhere else it's unknown, never no.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
